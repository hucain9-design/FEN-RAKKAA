Strategic Project Development Proposal

Project Fen Rakkaa — Governing the US Dollar Exposure of Male' Water and Sewerage Company

Hussain Shaany · S2610358 · Lecturer: Mr. Hussain Haleem · 13 August 2026 · Word count 4,416

1. Introduction

This document presents a strategic project development proposal for Male' Water and Sewerage Company (MWSC). The Business Plan 2026 identifies the USD exchange rate crisis as its top corporate challenge: MWSC earns in Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR) but pays for imports in US Dollars (USD), and the widening gap now feeds directly into its cost base. The proposal analyses the gaps behind this exposure and proposes a project to close them.

The proposed project, Fen Rakkaa ("safeguarding water"), is a twelve-month, MVR 4.5 million programme with three thrusts: earn revenue in dollars, cut dollar spending, and set clear rules for foreign exchange (FX) risk.

The report identifies the organisation and the project's strategic importance, presents the proposal document, then evaluates three options, recommends the most feasible, and develops a Logical Framework Matrix (Logframe) and Risk Management Plan for it.

2. Organisation and Case for Change

2.1 Organisational Context

Established in 1995, MWSC is the pioneer water and sewerage utility in the Maldives and a state-owned enterprise, 80% owned by the Government and 20% by Hitachi, with a total of 267,000 shares (MWSC, 2025b). It runs eight operation centres including the Greater Male' region, serving over half the population, while Fenaka Corporation covers most outer islands. MWSC is also the country's sole domestic manufacturer of PVC and PE pipes, undertaking engineering projects for government and resort clients (MWSC/AGO, 2025).

  • Core purpose: "to help our communities lead a PURE life" (MWSC, 2025b).
  • Audited FY2024: revenue MVR 1.58 billion; net profit MVR 217.5 million (MWSC/AGO, 2025).
  • Business Plan 2026: targets revenue of MVR 1.6 billion and net profit of MVR 186.9 million, and to "build strong inroads toward US dollar revenue streams" while maintaining reliable utility services (MWSC, 2025a).

FY2024 is the latest audited annual report; FY2025 appears as audited comparatives in the Q2 2026 report; in-year 2026 figures are unaudited. All figures are Company rather than Group, for consistency.

2.2 The Strategic Problem

The parallel-market rate recently reached MVR 22.25 per USD, a 44.29% premium above the official MVR 15.42 (Maldives Monetary Authority, 2026), rising steadily in a structural, not temporary, imbalance (Adhadhu, 2026). External debt service reached an estimated USD 1.7 billion in 2026, and official reserves fell from a peak of USD 1.33 billion in March 2026 to USD 717.9 million (1.4 months of import cover) in April after a USD 500 million sukuk repayment and currency swap; gross reserves continued to deteriorate to USD 638.03 million by July 2026 (Maldives Monetary Authority, 2026; Ministry of Finance and Planning, 2026; World Bank, 2026).

MWSC earns in MVR while key inputs (membranes, tanks, pumps, valves, meters) are bought in USD, so it is constantly exposed to currency shortages and rate pressure. Audited FY2024 disclosures show trade receivables of MVR 877.8 million against cash of MVR 50.2 million, a MVR 192.1 million impairment on gross receivables of MVR 1.23 billion flagged as a Key Audit Matter, and net finance costs up to MVR 55.9 million (MWSC/AGO, 2025).

The trajectory confirms this: audited comparatives show FY2025 revenue of MVR 1.37 billion (13% below 2024, only 62% of the MVR 2.2 billion goal) and profit after tax of MVR 146 million, about a third of 2024 (MWSC, 2026). At H1 2026, revenue of MVR 749.3 million was 6.4% below budget, the gap widening from 1.9% (Q1) to 10.9% (Q2).

2.3 Strategic Contribution and Cost of Inaction

Fen Rakkaa is of paramount strategic importance because it directly addresses the misalignment between the currency in which MWSC earns and the currency in which it spends. It is not merely a treasury adjustment; it is a fundamental requirement for the company's financial resilience, for three key reasons:

  • Strategic Alignment: it is the primary vehicle for three goals the Business Plan already sets but has not yet operationalised: USD billing (Goal 2.3.4), a USD share of the cash reserve (Goal 1.5.10) and legal review of foreign-currency contracts (Goal 3.3.2). The intended benefit is a structural reduction in net FX exposure that compounds year on year.
  • Financial Protection: the consequence of no action is an unmanaged annual exposure estimated at MVR 40–70 million (A2), together with the operational risk that USD shortages interrupt imports of chemicals and spares and threaten water supply itself.
  • Capability Building: it creates treasury discipline and contract governance that persist after the project closes and are difficult for others to copy (Barney, 1991), strengthening the financial element of the strategy mix (Callahan and Brooks, 2008).

3.2 Key Issues Analysis

Root-Cause Analysis

An evidence-based diagnosis must separate what is visible from what drives it. The falling margins, tight cash and rising finance costs in Section 2.2 are symptoms; the underlying problem is the structural mismatch between rufiyaa income and dollar costs. Four internal causes sustain that mismatch, in order of priority:

  • Under-developed dollar revenue: the Business Plan records the 150-plus resort sector and international consulting as overlooked markets. Ranked first because dollar income is a natural hedge: every rufiyaa converted to dollars shrinks the net exposure the other three causes each enlarge.
  • No treasury controls: budgets are prepared at the official MVR 15.42, there is no exposure register, and no dollar buffer is held, so the company plans at a rate it cannot transact at.
  • Contract design: project contracts are fixed-price in rufiyaa without FX adjustment clauses, so MWSC silently absorbs currency movements it never priced into the bid.
  • No ownership: no single function owns FX risk, which is why causes 2 and 3 persist. Fixing processes without fixing ownership is single-loop learning; the project targets the governing assumption itself, which is double-loop learning (Argyris, 1977).

Wishbone (Fishbone) Analysis

The causes are structured across six categories: Macroeconomic Environment, Revenue Structure, Financial and Treasury Practices, Procurement and Supply Chain, Contracts and Governance, and Capability and Culture. Only the first sits outside MWSC's control; the remaining five are internal and therefore actionable. The categories are interdependent, so the options in Section 4 are distinguished by how many linked causes each resolves rather than by how much capital each spends (Ishikawa, 1986).

4. Strategic Project Plan

4.1 Project Options and Strategies

Three genuinely distinct options were evaluated, from a status-quo baseline through a do-minimum fix to full transformation. A baseline is included so the value of intervention is tested rather than assumed.

  • Option A: "Do Nothing" (Baseline) — continue budgeting at the official MVR 15.42 and absorb losses. Its true cost is the full A2 exposure; it appears safe while failing every strategic objective, which is why doing nothing is the most expensive option over time.
  • Option B: "The Treasury Fix" (Do-Minimum) — a realistic planning rate, consolidated orders and a basic dollar buffer, ~MVR 1.5 million. Cheap and fast, but revenue stays entirely in rufiyaa, so the root cause is untouched.
  • Option C: "The Full Fen Rakkaa Programme" (Integrated) — six coordinated workstreams covering governance, dollar revenue, treasury, procurement, contracts and energy savings, ~MVR 4.5 million over twelve months under two stage gates. The only option addressing all four root causes; financing is achievable, and the staged approach allows a priced de-scope to Option B if benefits are not realised.

4.2 Financial Appraisal and Sensitivity

Benefits ramp at 15%, 35% and 45% of the MVR 55 million exposure (A2) over three years, against MVR 4.5m one-off and MVR 1.2m annual costs at a 12% discount. Three-year NPV is MVR 33.4 million, benefit-cost ratio 5.8, payback within Year 1. An IRR is not quoted: at this cost scale it would imply false precision (HM Treasury, 2022).

The recommendation is robust to reasonable changes. Doubling the cost weight to 30% and cutting strategic fit to 5% still leaves Option C ranked first (4.00 vs 3.40 and 3.05). The programme breaks even at just 17% of estimated benefits and remains positive at 40% even with running costs over by two-thirds; Gate 2 is set at 35%, above break-even. A change to the peg would strengthen, not weaken, the case for FX governance.

4.3 Recommendation

Option C, the Full Fen Rakkaa Programme, is unequivocally recommended, with Option B retained as a priced fallback should funding be cut. It provides the best available balance of strategic alignment, financial return and risk control, and is the only option that resolves every root cause. The accepted trade-off is additional workload in a crowded change year, managed by delivering through existing KPIs and governance. The principal limitation is that benefits rest on an assumed dollar-linked share of costs rather than published segment data, which is why the exposure register is the first deliverable and the programme is gated rather than committed in full. Because the environment is complex rather than predictable, the design probes, learns and adjusts at each gate (Snowden and Boone, 2007).

6. Conclusion

This proposal has set out a strategically vital project for MWSC. The causes the company can act on are internal, and the recommended Full Programme addresses all of them; the appraisal shows it outperforms both doing nothing and the partial fix on every weighting tested. The logframe and risk register state in advance how delivery will be measured, what failure would look like and what the organisation would do in response. Approval of Option C is therefore requested: a gated investment of roughly 0.3% of annual revenue set against an unmanaged exposure running into the tens of millions.

Why this design

The introduction makes exactly three moves and stops: problem, project, roadmap. It does not argue the case yet; that is Section 2's job. The three-thrust framing (earn, cut, govern) is the spine of the whole document; every workstream, objective and risk hangs off one of the three.

Where the problem statement comes from

The claim that this is the top corporate challenge is not your judgement, it is MWSC's own: the Business Plan 2026 lists the USD exchange rate crisis first among corporate challenges (MWSC, 2025a). That sourcing matters because it means the project responds to the organisation's stated priority, not an invented one.

Q: Why this project and not something bigger, like a desalination expansion?
A: Because the Business Plan itself ranks the currency crisis as challenge number one, and capital projects are downstream of it: every capital project gets more expensive while the FX problem is unmanaged. Fixing the pricing of everything else comes before building more of anything.
Q: What does Fen Rakkaa mean and why a Dhivehi name?
A: It means safeguarding water. The name signals that this is not a finance-department side project; currency risk now threatens the core mission, which is water security.
Q: In one sentence, what is the project?
A: A twelve-month, four-and-a-half-million-rufiyaa programme that reduces MWSC's net dollar exposure by earning dollars, spending fewer dollars, and putting permanent governance around FX risk.

2.2 The Strategic Problem

What it says

Two layers. National: the parallel rate hit a record MVR 22.25 on 11 August 2026, a 44.29% premium over the 15.42 peg, rising year on year (structural, not a spike); external debt service around USD 1.7 billion in 2026; reserves fell to about USD 718 million after the USD 500 million sukuk repayment in April 2026, near 1.4 months of import cover; banks rationing dollars. Company: MWSC earns MVR, buys membranes, tanks, pumps, valves, meters in USD; audited strain markers (cash 50.2m vs receivables 877.8m; 192.1m impairment as a Key Audit Matter; net finance costs 55.9m); trajectory (FY2025 revenue 1.37bn, −13%, only 62% of the 2.2bn goal; PAT 146m, about a third of 2024; H1 2026 revenue 6.4% under budget, gap widening 1.9% to 10.9%).

Why structured national-then-company

The national layer proves the problem is real and external; the company layer proves MWSC is specifically exposed and already bleeding. Without the first, the project looks paranoid; without the second, it looks like someone else's problem.

How the trajectory argument works

It is a three-point downward line: full-year 2025 worse than 2024, H1 2026 worse than its own budget, and the gap widening quarter on quarter inside 2026. Three independent measurements pointing the same way is what makes "structural, not a spike" defensible.

Evidence, precisely

  • Debt service ~USD 1.7bn and 1.4 months import cover: World Bank (2026); reserves after the sukuk: Ministry of Finance and Planning (2026).
  • Bank restrictions: Maldives Independent (2026), 23 June article.
  • Cash 50.2m, receivables 877.8m, impairment 192.1m (KAM), finance costs 55.9m: MWSC/AGO (2025), the KAM at p.40.

A distinction to hold ready

The fishbone shows "~USD 1.0bn debt service remaining 2026" while the text says USD 1.7 billion for 2026. Both are right: 1.7 is the full-year estimate, roughly 1.0 was what remained at mid-year after the April sukuk repayment. If anyone probes the difference — total versus remaining.

Q: The parallel market is technically illegal. Why is it valid evidence?
A: Because it is the price at which dollars actually clear. The audited accounts and quarterly margins corroborate that MWSC's real costs move with that rate, not the official one. Planning at 15.42 when procurement happens near 22 is the fiction; the tracker is the reality.
Q: Could the FY2025 squeeze be caused by something other than FX, like demand?
A: Partly, and I do not claim FX explains everything. That is why the impact indicator in the logframe is FX loss avoided rather than company profit: the project only claims the slice it controls. But the gross margin falling 7.5 points inside six months while dollar input prices rise is exactly the FX signature.
Q: What if the government devalues the peg?
A: Then the numbers change but the case strengthens: a repriced peg makes FX governance more necessary, not less. That is R1 in the register, with a banded planning rate and a 30-day re-baseline trigger already designed.

2.3 Strategic Contribution & Cost of Inaction (Table 1)

What it says

The case for change: paramount strategic importance, not merely a treasury adjustment, for three reasons — Strategic Alignment (delivers plan Goals 2.3.4, 1.5.10, 3.3.2), Financial Protection (inaction costs MVR 40–70 million a year plus operational risk to water supply), Capability Building (durable, hard-to-copy capabilities on the financial element of the strategy mix). Then Table 1, the assumptions register: A1 rate, A2 exposure, A3 benefits.

Why three reasons, and these three

They cover the three tests any investment case faces: does it fit strategy, does it protect money, does it build something lasting. Alignment cites the plan's own goal numbers so the marker can verify the fit in the source. Protection quantifies the do-nothing cost, which makes Option A comparable later. Capability invokes the resource-based view: what survives the project is the point.

Evidence

Goals 2.3.4 (dollar billing), 1.5.10 (USD share of cash reserve), 3.3.2 (legal review of FX contracts): MWSC (2025a), pp.26, 32 and 40. Capability theory: Barney (1991); strategy mix: Callahan and Brooks (2008).

How each assumption was built

A1, rate 22.25 (band 22.00–22.50). Source Adhadhu, 11 Aug 2026; premium 22.25/15.42 = 1.4429 → 44.3%. The band is built from observed drift: the rate opened 2026 at 20.50 and reached 22.25 by August, ~0.25/month, so the band is the observed rate ± one month of average drift (±1.1%), reviewed quarterly with a 30-day re-baseline on any peg announcement (R1). Robustness is tested separately at ±10% (Appendix A: a 10% rate fall ≈ 48% realisation, still nearly 3× break-even).

A2, exposure, planning estimate MVR 55m p.a. Step one, base: 2024 opex MVR 935.3m (p.34) × 20–25% dollar-linked = MVR 187–234m. Step two, two bases: against the peg at 44.3% = 83–104m; as incremental deterioration against 2024 realised rates of ~MVR 18 (a 23.5% uplift to today's parallel rate) = 44–55m. Step three, the choice: 55m is the ceiling of the conservative realised-rate base, not a midpoint. Anything above 55 needs weight on the peg base (overstates for a company that has not transacted at the peg for years); anything below ignores that rates kept deteriorating since 2024. Averaging the two bases gives 71.5m, so adopting 55 deliberately leaves about a quarter of the arguable exposure out of the case. Full derivation in Appendix B.

A3, benefits 25–40m at maturity. A capture band on A2. Floor: 45% of 55 = 24.75 → 25. Ceiling: ~73% capture with all four objectives delivered, 0.73 × 55 ≈ 40; never 100%, because the national scarcity and the government's rufiyaa contracts stay outside reach. Bottom-up cross-check: procurement anchored to MWSC's own 5% savings goal on the dollar-linked base, 5% × 187–234m = MVR 9–12m; the rest is premium avoided on dollar income and margins protected by FX clauses.

Why "planning estimate", not "central"

Earlier drafts called 55 central; against the arithmetic that word fails — the midpoint of the two bases is 71.5, and 55 is the centre of nothing computable. The final wording says what is true: 55 is the ceiling of the conservative realised-rate base, 23% below the blended midpoint. That gives the strongest line in the financial defence: had the two bases simply been averaged the model would run on 71.5m; it runs on 55, so the NPV of 33.4m already excludes roughly a quarter of the defensible exposure — and the case still breaks even at 17% of it.

Alternatives rejected

Published segment data: does not exist, MWSC does not disclose cost by currency. Commissioning a study first: delays action and duplicates the project's own first deliverable. Peg base alone: overstates; realised base alone: understates; presenting both and choosing conservatively is the defensible middle.

Q: Your whole financial case rests on an assumed 20–25%. How confident are you?
A: It is the softest input in the document and I say so in three places. That is exactly why the exposure register is the first deliverable: it replaces the assumption with measured values, and Gate 2 re-tests the business case on real data at month twelve. I am not asking the board to trust the estimate; I am asking them to fund the instrument that removes it.
Q: You call 55 a planning estimate. Is it not just the top of your lower range? Why not the midpoint, 71.5?
A: Deliberately the top of the lower range. The realised base is the honest measurement method, because it only counts deterioration beyond what MWSC actually paid; its ceiling is the highest figure the cautious method supports. The midpoint of both bases would be 71.5, and choosing 55 instead leaves about a quarter of the arguable exposure out of the case on purpose. The model then breaks even at 17% of even that reduced figure.
Q: Where does 40–70 in the text come from if A2 says 55?
A: 40–70 is the working range across both bases rounded for prose; 55 is the modelling figure. Range for honesty, point for arithmetic.
Q: Why is the planning band only 22.00–22.50 when you stress-test ±10%?
A: Two instruments, two jobs. The band is what budgets and costings use — the observed rate plus one month of this year's average drift, reviewed quarterly. The ten per cent is how far the model was pushed to prove the recommendation survives. A narrow band with a review cycle is operational discipline; the wide range is analytical honesty. One number cannot do both jobs.

A: It is a capture band on the 55 million exposure. The floor is what Year 3 already assumes, 45%, which is 25. The ceiling is about 73% capture with everything delivered, roughly 40, and it is never 100% because the national scarcity and the government's rufiyaa book stay outside reach. 3.1 Scope & Objectives (Tables 2 & 3)

Why a scope statement table

It is the standard PM artefact; the brief's definition checklist (objectives, deliverables, boundaries, constraints, assumptions) can be verified at a glance; and the acceptance-criteria row formally separates "delivered" from "successful," which is the distinction the stage gates run on.

Element-by-element rationale

  • Goal: "net" exposure because dollar revenue offsets spending; 0.29% of revenue is the proportionality argument; no new permanent staff kills the SOE headcount objection and keeps recurring cost at 1.2m.
  • Deliverables: register/dashboard fixes measurement (kills A2 softness); billing framework + USD account + buffer is the revenue-side machinery and R2's mitigation; planning-rate directive kills cause 2; clause library kills cause 3 and is reusable infrastructure.
  • Acceptance criteria: binary and verifiable — dashboard live at 100% coverage; buffer policy EXCO-approved; directive issued; library in 100% of new tenders. Deliberately "issued" not "complied with": acceptance tests existence, objectives test effect.
  • Boundaries: in scope is enterprise-wide because the fishbone shows fragmentation across divisions; each out-of-scope item has its own reason — the peg (feasibility), speculative hedging (prudence; the buffer is protective inventory, not a position), tariffs (ethics), capital delivery (focus).
  • Constraints vs dependencies: constraints are walls you design around (rationed USD, MVR government contracts, the cap, SOE approvals); dependencies are supports others must provide (analytics, procurement digitalisation, Legal, banks) — they reappear as logframe assumptions. The assumptions row restates A1 correctly (procurement rate 22.25 in band, not "peg holds") and carries A2's self-correction clause.

Table 3 SMART — why these four

They are MWSC's own Business Plan KPIs adopted, not invented: O1 register/dashboard + 20% USD reserve (Goal 1.5.10), O2 USD billing 50% of resorts / 20% of project revenue (Goal 2.3.4), O3 procurement down 10–15% volume-adjusted, O4 100% legal review with FX clauses (Goal 3.3.2). Volume-adjusted in O3 matters: it prevents claiming savings that are really just lower activity.

Q: Difference between your acceptance criteria and your objectives?
A: Acceptance confirms the deliverable exists and is adopted; objectives confirm the business changed. You can pass acceptance and miss the objective. Gate 2 exists precisely to test the second after the first.
Q: Why is there no headcount in a project this size?
A: Deliberate. It is a governance and behaviour project delivered through existing divisions with part-time champions. That keeps recurring cost to 1.2 million a year and means the capability lives in the line, not in a project team that disbands.
Q: Why only 50% of resorts, why not all?
A: New contracts only, by design, to avoid repudiating existing agreements, and because resorts hold bargaining power. Fifty per cent by June 2027 with the FX-indexed rufiyaa fallback (R3) is ambitious but honest.

3.2 Key Issues Analysis (RCA, Fishbone, Gap)

Three tools, divided work

RCA explains why the exposure persists; the fishbone structures and weights the causes and shows five of six categories are internal, therefore actionable; the gap analysis states what must exist to close each one, which becomes the scope the options must deliver. Triangulation, not repetition: each tool answers a different question.

Why this cause ranking

Dollar revenue first because it is the natural hedge: every dollar earned shrinks the net exposure the other three causes each enlarge. Treasury second because planning at 15.42 while transacting near 22 poisons every budget. Contracts third because fixed-price MVR bids absorb unpriced risk. Ownership fourth but foundational — it is why 2 and 3 persist. The Argyris move: fixing processes without fixing ownership is single-loop; targeting the governing assumption (who owns FX risk) is double-loop (Argyris, 1977).

The inference logic, in full

For cause 1 there is direct evidence: the plan records resorts and international consulting as overlooked markets (MWSC, 2025a, p.7). For causes 2 and 3 no document says "we lack controls," because companies do not publish lists of missing controls. So the absence is inferred from the plan's forward goals: a 2026 goal to create dollar billing, a reserve and contract review is evidence those controls do not yet exist. The audited accounts corroborate: budgets struck at the peg and no disclosed FX buffer are consistent with absence, not refinement.

Why custom fishbone categories, not the 6 Ms

The 6 Ms are a manufacturing template; a currency problem has no machine or material dimension. The brief requires the diagram tailored and interpreted. The six categories used (Macroeconomic Environment, Revenue Structure, Financial and Treasury Practices, Procurement and Supply Chain, Contracts and Governance, Capability and Culture) map one-to-one onto the RCA plus the external boundary, so the two tools lock together (Ishikawa, 1986).

Gap analysis — the design claim

Every gap is a capability, behaviour or control, never an asset. That single observation is the thesis: the project buys nothing physical; it builds what persists. Each future-state cell also names the mechanism that closes the gap (directive, templates, account, account team, clause library, committee, KPIs), connecting diagnosis to delivery.

Q: A goal to create a control could mean strengthening an existing one, not building from zero.
A: The plan frames these as new capabilities to establish, and the audited accounts corroborate absence: budgets at the peg rate and no disclosed buffer are consistent with nothing being there. If a partial control existed, strengthening it is still within scope; the diagnosis does not change.
Q: Why is the national shortage not in your root causes?
A: It is a cause, but external. MWSC cannot fix the country's reserves, so it is treated as a planning assumption and a risk (R2), while the RCA keeps to causes the project can actually close. Diagnosing what you cannot treat is not analysis, it is commentary.
Q: Your fishbone departs from the standard categories. Why?
A: The tool is designed to be adapted; the standard Ms are a factory template. I tailored the categories to this problem's causal structure and mapped them to the RCA so the tools triangulate. The brief explicitly requires a tailored, interpreted diagram.

3.3 Stakeholder Analysis (Figure 3, Table 5)

Why salience on top of power–interest

Power–interest alone would file the monetary authority as medium-interest and forget it; salience captures that its claim is urgent and legitimate, because it controls the dollars the buffer needs. The Mitchell test (power, legitimacy, urgency) catches actors the 2×2 underweights.

Why every quadrant is populated, and by whom

Manage closely: Board/MD, CFO, Finance Ministry/PCB, client ministry, monetary authority and banks. Keep informed: resorts/B2B, internal divisions. Keep satisfied: Auditor General/AGO, because the audited accounts underpin the evidence base and audit findings can validate or discredit reported benefits — engagement is a full audit trail and Internal Audit verification before gates. Monitor: domestic customers/public, little formal power but they anchor the ethical boundary; the engagement action is the commitment itself, no change to household billing. An empty half-grid invites the question "did you look?"; this composition answers it.

Why evidenced/inferred flags

Positions like the Board's support (sponsoring office changed mid-2026) and the AGO's independence (they flagged the Key Audit Matter) are evidenced. Positions like ministry conditionality or resort resistance are inferences, marked (I) and re-assessed at each gate. The same honesty device as A1–A3: say what you know versus what you assume.

Q: Several positions are inferred. How confident are you?
A: The table says exactly which ones, and the design assumes I might be wrong: positions are re-assessed at each gate, and the riskiest inference, resort resistance, has a priced fallback in R3 — FX-indexed rufiyaa pricing if uptake is under 50%.
Q: Why is the Auditor General only keep-satisfied when your evidence depends on them?
A: High power, low day-to-day interest in this project: they audit annually, they do not manage change. Keep satisfied is precisely the right posture: full transparency, Internal Audit verifying benefits before gates, nothing that surprises them at year end.
Q: Is dollar billing ethical for an essential service?
A: For resorts and commercial clients who earn in dollars, yes; it prices reality. The ethical line is households, which is why tariffs are excluded from scope and domestic billing does not change. The stakeholder table carries that commitment in writing.

4.1 Options & Strategies (Table 6)

Why a do-nothing baseline

The brief mandates a do-minimum or baseline so the value of intervening is tested, not assumed. Option A also does real analytical work: it prices inaction (the A2 exposure) and exposes the trap that doing nothing looks safe while being the most expensive choice over time.

Why these weights

Strategic fit and benefits carry 20 each because the project exists to deliver the plan's top challenge and pay for itself. Feasibility and cost at 15 reflect SOE budget discipline. Declaring weights before scoring is the anti-rigging move; the sensitivity test then proves the ranking survives even hostile weights.

How the scores work

Option A scores 5 on cost and feasibility (doing little is easy) and 1 on fit, benefits, risk and sustainability (it achieves nothing); weighted 2.45. Option B is mid-table 3s and 4s, cheap and quick but capped; 3.25. Option C: 5 on fit, benefits, risk reduction, sustainability, 4 feasibility, 3 cost and time; 4.30. The pattern answer for any single cell: the score follows from how many root causes the option touches.

Funding realism

MWSC raised MVR 111.15 million through a private bond at a fixed 7.5% in 2025 (MWSC/AGO, 2025, p.39). If the company can raise 111 million on the market, it can fund 4.5 million for the programme that protects the currency those bondholders are repaid in.

Alternatives rejected as options

Full hedging strategy: excluded from scope on prudence grounds, so it cannot be an option. Tariff-led recovery: excluded on ethics. A capital project (e.g. solar to cut fuel imports): energy savings appear as a workstream inside C, but a capital-led option would answer a different question at a different price.

Q: Are A, B and C genuinely distinct, or is B just C minus some scope?
A: Distinct in kind, not just size. A changes nothing. B changes treasury behaviour only, all income stays in rufiyaa. C changes the revenue model itself. The dividing line is whether the root cause — no dollar income — is addressed; only C crosses it.
Q: Who scored the matrix and how do I know it is not rigged?
A: Weights were fixed before scoring and are justified criterion by criterion. Then I attacked my own weighting: doubling cost to 30% and cutting strategic fit to 5% still leaves C first at 4.00 against 3.40 and 3.05. The ranking is not an artefact of the weights.
Q: Why is Option C only 3 on cost when 4.5m is trivial against the exposure?
A: The criterion scores absolute outlay against alternatives, and C is triple B's cost, so B outranks it there. Value for money is captured in the benefits criterion and the NPV, not double-counted in cost.

4.2 Financial Appraisal & Sensitivity (Figure 4)

How the model computes, line by line

Year 1: benefit 8.25 (15% of 55), cost 5.70 (4.5+1.2), net 2.55, DF 0.8929, PV 2.28. Year 2: 19.25 / 1.20 / 18.05 / 0.7972 / 14.39. Year 3: 24.75 / 1.20 / 23.55 / 0.7118 / 16.76. NPV 33.43; PV benefits 40.33, PV costs 6.90, BCR 5.84; break-even = 6.90/40.33 = 17.1%.

Why 15/35/45 as the ramp

Year 1 is mostly build (register, account, directive), so benefits are small; Year 2 the billing and procurement changes bite; Year 3 approaches but does not reach maturity. Never modelling full capture is itself conservatism.

Why 12% discount, and no IRR

12% is a deliberately high hurdle for a rufiyaa cash flow, absorbing SOE risk and estimate softness; a lower rate would only raise the NPV. No IRR because at this cost scale it computes to an absurd percentage implying precision the estimates do not have — the Green Book's position is that NPV is the decision metric and IRR can mislead exactly here (HM Treasury, 2022). Quoting a 300% IRR would damage credibility, not build it.

How the A1 rate test maps onto Figure 4

A 10% rate fall (to 20.03) cuts the realised premium from 23.5% to 11.3%, roughly halving the modelled exposure — the same as about 48% realisation on the chart: NPV near 12 million, still almost three times above break-even. A 10% rise (to 24.48) lifts exposure by about half. Every input error, rate included, expresses itself on the realisation axis, which is why that is the axis Figure 4 sweeps.

Why Gate 2 sits at 35%

Above the 17% break-even with buffer, and equal to the Year 2 ramp assumption, so the gate tests the model against its own milestone using its own arithmetic. If realisation is under 35% at month twelve, de-scope to Option B, which is already priced. The continuation decision and the financial model are the same test.

Q: Only three years. Why not five?
A: Three years is the honest horizon for estimate quality, and the case already clears at three; extending the tail would only inflate an NPV that does not need inflating. The capabilities persist beyond year three, which is upside, not baseline.
Q: What single number would kill this project?
A: Realisation below 17% of estimated benefits, sustained. That is the break-even, it is printed on Figure 4, and Gate 2 is set above it precisely so the project cannot drift past its own kill-line unexamined.

4.3 Recommendation

Why name trade-offs and a limitation inside the recommendation

The rubric's exemplary band demands trade-offs, uncertainties and limitations explained, not hidden. A recommendation that admits its weakest point and shows the mechanism that manages it (gates, fallback) reads as judgement; one that claims certainty reads as salesmanship. Option C is recommended, Option B retained as the priced fallback; the accepted trade-off is workload in a crowded change year, managed through existing KPIs; the rejected trade-off is Option A's apparent safety, its losses hiding as gradual currency erosion.

Why Cynefin here

Snowden and Boone's point is that in complex domains you probe, sense, respond rather than plan-then-execute. That is the intellectual justification for gates and re-baselining rather than a fixed three-year commitment. One sentence, load-bearing.

Q: If funding is halved mid-year, what survives?
A: Option B is the priced answer: the directive, consolidated imports and the buffer, about 1.5 million. That is also exactly the R6 trigger response, so the de-scope path is designed, not improvised.

4.4 Logframe (Figure 5, Table 7)

Why impact is FX loss avoided, not profit

The project does not control profit; demand, tariffs and weather do. Claiming profit would break the causal chain the LFA exists to protect. Claiming only the FX slice keeps every link defensible.

Why assumptions sit at specific levels

An assumption belongs at the level whose achievement it threatens: peg stability threatens the impact, not the activities; SAP data quality threatens outputs. Placing them correctly is what makes the logframe a risk instrument rather than a form. Gate 1 (month 6) tests tools are live; Gate 2 (month 12) tests 35% realisation.

Q: Walk me up the chain from one activity to impact.
A: Take the clause library. Activity: Legal drafts and issues it. Output: 100% of new tenders carry FX clauses, verified in tender audits. Outcome: new project revenue stops silently absorbing currency movements. Impact: measured FX loss against the do-nothing baseline falls. Each link has an indicator, a verification source and an owner in the table.

5. Risk Management Plan (Table 8)

The register, one line each with its logic

  • R1 Strategic 15: thin reserves → peg adjusted → exposure repriced. Banded rate, quarterly review; trigger: announcement → re-baseline in 30 days. Top because it invalidates the arithmetic itself.
  • R2 Financial 15: FX rationing → dollars unavailable → production threatened. Multi-bank, safety stock; trigger: refusal → draw buffer, escalate. The water-security risk.
  • R3 Stakeholder 12: resorts refuse USD billing → outcome missed. New contracts only; trigger <50% uptake Q1 2027 → FX-indexed rufiyaa pricing. Keeps the economics while conceding the currency.
  • R4 Governance 12: clauses rejected or sponsorship lapses → margins exposed. FIDIC precedent, re-secure sponsorship; trigger: rejection → ring-fence funds.
  • R5 Delivery 9: change fatigue → benefits slip past Gate 2. Embed in KPIs; trigger: two missed milestones → re-phase.
  • R6 Financial 8: estimated baselines → benefits overstated → case weakens. Conservative baselines, Internal Audit; trigger: <35% at Gate 2 → de-scope to B. The hinge risk: its trigger is the gate and its response the already-priced fallback, so governance and finance meet in one row.
  • R7 Operational 8: consolidated imports → mis-forecast → spares stock-out. Criticality classes, min-max; trigger: A-class stock-out → air-freight.
  • R8 Sustainability 6: discipline lapses after close → benefits erode. Corporate register, divisional KPIs; trigger: two quarters unreviewed → reinstate committee.

Why cause–event–effect phrasing

It forces each risk to be a causal claim you can act on at three points: remove the cause, detect the event, limit the effect. "Currency risk" as a label is unmanageable; "thin reserves lead to peg adjustment lead to repricing" is. Programme-level governance (Barclay and Osei-Bryson, 2015); method ISO 31000 (ISO, 2018).

Q: Which risk worries you most?
A: R2, because it is the one that touches water supply, and its levers are partly outside MWSC. That is why the buffer exists, why banking is multi-bank, and why the escalation is framed as water security, which changes how the authorities hear it.
Q: Why does nothing in the register overturn your recommendation?
A: Only R1 to R4 could, and each carries a fallback preserving the core logic: re-baseline, buffer, indexed pricing, ring-fenced funds. The register was built to test the recommendation, and the recommendation survived it; that is what the caption states.

6. Conclusion

Causes are internal and all addressed; the appraisal outperforms both alternatives on every weighting tested; the logframe and register pre-state how delivery is measured and what happens on failure; approval requested — a gated 0.3% of revenue against an exposure in the tens of millions. It closes on the asymmetry, and that final sentence is the whole case compressed: small, controlled, reversible spend against large, unmanaged, compounding loss. If you get one sentence in the defence, it is this one.
